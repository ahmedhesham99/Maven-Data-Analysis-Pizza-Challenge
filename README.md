# Maven-Data-Analysis-Pizza-Challenge


## Project Objective:
- Sales Performance: Analyze revenues through the year and pizzas performance.
- Operations Performance: Analyze the peak periods to see how well the restaurant is utilizing the seating capacity of 15 tables (60 total seats).

## Tools Used:
Data cleaning, processing and quick analysis were performed using **Python (Pandas)**, while final analysis, visualization and dashboarding were prototyped in **Power BI**.

## Key Insights:
- **Over all Financial Health:**
  - The business generated a **total revenue of $816,852** from **21,350 unique orders**, resulting in the sale of **50,000 pizza units**.
  - Average Order Value: **The AOV stands at $38.26.** This value is strong, because customers frequently purchase  larger pizzas per transaction.
  - Average Order Quantity: **2.32 pizzas per order.** (This high average is critical, confirming orders are often for groups or multiple meals, requiring high-capacity assembly.)
- **Product Performance:**
  - Top Category: **The Classic category** is the undisputed revenue leader, This category consistently performs well, because it fulfills the need for fast, affordable, crowd-pleasing comfort food. While The Veggie was the lowest.
  - Top Selling Size (Volume): **Large, selling 18,934 units.** This confirms that the majority of demand is for large, sharable pizzas.
  - Top Selling Pizza: **The Thai Chicken Pizza** is the single highest-grossing item, generating **$43,372.00.** This suggests a strong customer appetite for premium and specialty items when executed well.
  - Worst Selling Pizza (Revenue): **The Brie Carre Pizza ($11,588.50)**. This low performer indicates either niche appeal, pricing issues, or lack of promotion, requiring review or maybe problems in ingredients.
- **Key Seasonal Trends:**
  - Highest Revenue Month: **July ($72,557.90)**. This confirms peak summer demand, likely driven by tourism, holidays, or warm-weather gatherings and the heat of the weather.
  - Worst Revenue Month: **October** is the worst-performing month, recording a *revenue of $63,750.85.* This may be because of consumer budget fatigue, and pre-holiday savings mode.
  - Highest Volume Day: **Friday,** the last working day, **had the highest orders (3538)** because of the need for socializing, celebration, and max convenience. While Sunday had the lowest orders (2624) because it is the peak preparation day, naturally suppressing takeout demand.
- **Operational strain points:** 
  - Peak Hour: **12 PM (Noon)** (5,815 Pizzas in 2,520 Orders).
  - In general rush hours were **at lunch and dinner** times at 12 PM, 1 PM, 5 PM , 6 PM, With an average of  **6.79 orders per hour**.
- **Seating Capacity Utilization (15 Tables / 60 Seats):**
  - The restaurant's capacity of 15 orders per hour was exceeded **7 times**.
  - The most frequent overload hours were 6 PM and 1 PM, each accounting for 2 incidents.
  - During overload incidents, the average number of unique orders was 17.43.



## Recommendations
- **Trough October:** Implement a targeted promotion (like the "Fall Family Value Pack") to specifically boost traffic and revenue during this annual slow period.
- **Leverage Top Performer:** Create a limited-time promotional bundle featuring the Thai Chicken Pizza (your top revenue generator) paired with a new appetizer or dessert item.
- **Maximize Peak Month:** Initiate customers reviews in May/June to ensure maximum quality and satisfaction for the July peak.
- **Keep the Loyal customers:** Build loyalty programs to give them small gifts and let them get new customers.
- **Highlight and promote the Large (L) size**, solidify it as the go-to "shareable" choice, and further reinforce your strong Average Order Value (AOV).
- **Maintain Veggie Coverage:** Introduce one new, limited-time Veggie pizza per quarter to capture the loyalty of this specialized, high-spending segment.
- **Review the Weak Link:** Try to get customer reviews for The Brie Carre Pizza (worst seller) to know if the reason is lack of marketing or poor portions.
- **Staffing Adjustments:** Increase staffing levels during the peak hours of 12 PM, 1 PM, 5 PM, and 6 PM, especially on Fridays, and consider advanced ingredient preparation to be completed before.
- **Maximize Peak Month:** Initiate inventory and staffing reviews in May/June to ensure maximum capacity for the July peak.
- **Menu Optimization:** During peak times, simplifying the menu or focusing on faster-to-prepare and items and pause the lowest volume time consuming pizzas could help manage order volume without compromising quality.
- **Pre-order System:** Implementing or promoting a pre-order system could help smooth out demand fluctuations.
- **Prioritize the Classic Category:** Create a dedicated, permanently stocked assembly station for the Classic category. Since these are the highest volume and simplest pizzas, they should be routed through the fastest lane to minimize overall queue time.
- **Optimize Large-Size Production:** Implement a system to pre-form 80% of the expected large-sized dough for lunch and dinner rushes. This shifts the slow, manual task of dough shaping out of the critical peak period.
- **Capacity Review:** Consider the ability to take 5 tables for temporary usage in the production area based on the assumption that most orders were for delivery because of the large size and supreme category high volume.
- In conclusion, while not a constant issue, the identified overload periods represent critical junctures where operational adjustments could enhance efficiency and customer satisfaction.
